Devices that move the cursor for you

GazeWheel only needs one thing from your equipment: cursor movement. This is the equipment side — from built-in, zero-cost options to dedicated assistive eye trackers. Start with what you already have; upgrade when you hit its limits.

Already in your device built in

Try these first. They cost nothing and need no extra hardware.

Apple iPad / iPhone — Eye Tracking

Built into iPadOS and iOS accessibility settings since 2024: the front camera tracks your eyes and a dwell click selects. Works with GazeWheel in Safari on the same screen.

Windows — Eye Control

Built into Windows 10/11 (Settings → Accessibility). Needs a supported eye tracker connected, then moves the cursor and clicks by dwell system-wide.

A trackball, joystick or any adapted mouse

If any body part can move a large trackball or a joystick, that is already enough. No special software needed.

Free software using a webcam free

Head or eye tracking with the camera you already own. Less precise than dedicated hardware, good for testing whether cursor typing works for you.

Camera Mouse / Enable Viacam

Free head tracking: the webcam follows your head movement and moves the cursor. Windows.

GazePointer

Free webcam eye tracking for Windows — moves the cursor with your gaze, no extra hardware.

OptiKey

Free, open-source on-screen keyboard and mouse control built for eye trackers — useful to control the rest of the computer beyond writing.

Dedicated eye trackers

Purpose-built assistive hardware: precise, works in bed and in changing light, with professional support. This is the tier Tobii is known for.

Tobii Dynavox — PCEye

Compact eye tracker for Windows computers and tablets, screens up to 27". With Windows Control software it clicks for you — with GazeWheel, keep dwell off in our settings and let it do the clicking. This is the setup GazeWheel was designed around.

Tobii Dynavox — TD I-Series / TD Pilot

Complete speech-generating devices (dedicated tablet + eye tracker + mounting), including a version built around the iPad. The full-package route, often covered by insurance or public health programs.

Irisbond — Hiru

Eye tracker from Spain that works on both Windows and iPad. A strong Tobii alternative, common in Europe and Latin America.

EyeTech — TM5 Mini

US-made assistive eye tracker for Windows, known for working well with glasses and droopy eyelids.

Head mice

If your head still moves well, these are cheaper than eye tracking and very precise.

Quha Zono

Wireless gyroscopic mouse worn on the head (or cap, or glasses). Windows, Mac and Android.

GlassOuse

Glasses-shaped head mouse with a choice of switches (bite, blink, finger) for the click.
