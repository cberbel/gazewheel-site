Why round?

The common on-screen keyboard is a copy of the physical keyboard — a layout created for typing with 8–10 fingers. With a cursor you have one pointer, and speed is decided by how far it has to travel between keys. A circle is the natural shape for that: every key sits close to the center, so the average distance per selection is as short as it gets.

The full alphabet stays on the outer ring, always in the same position. The inner ring shows only the letters likely to come next, large and near the center. The center is a rest zone — a dead area where the cursor can park without triggering anything.

What it does

  • Word prediction that learns. Suggestions adapt to your vocabulary and your word chains. Accepting a word usually costs one selection instead of five or six. You can also feed it your own texts and export a backup.
  • It speaks for you. End a sentence with a period and it is spoken out loud automatically. An attention chime says "wait, I'm typing" so the other person doesn't walk away.
  • Conversation mode. Your text goes full-screen, large, facing the person you are talking to.
  • A real cursor. Move word by word through what you wrote and fix a word in the middle without erasing everything after it. Long texts stay editable.
  • Saved phrases for the things you say every day.
  • Rest zone and auto-pause. After each selection there is a short pause, so a parked cursor never fires twice.

It adapts to your setup

Everything the app needs from your equipment is cursor movement. If your device only moves the pointer, turn on dwell selection and set the dwell time. If it already clicks by itself (like Tobii Windows Control), keep dwell off so there are no two timers stacked. Wheel size, text size, letter order, contrast, voice and speed are all adjustable:

Who it is for

People with ALS, cerebral palsy, high spinal cord injury, brainstem stroke, advanced multiple sclerosis or muscular dystrophy — any situation where speech is gone and hands don't reach the keys.

Also for people temporarily without a voice: intubated or tracheostomized ICU patients can usually move one finger on a screen. This runs on any tablet the hospital already has.

Word prediction is currently in Portuguese.

Privacy

Nothing you write leaves your computer. Learned vocabulary, saved phrases and settings are stored in your own browser, and you can export a backup file at any time. No text is sent to any server.
